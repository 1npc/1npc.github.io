Language models are fluent about knowledge. Whether they can *operate* on it is a different question, and a harder one to ask, because most benchmarks reward a model for recognising a fact rather than for doing anything with it.

OntoURL asks the harder version. It takes 40 ontologies across 8 domains — formal, symbolic descriptions of a field in terms of classes, relations, properties, and instances — and builds 58,981 questions over them, spread across 15 tasks in three groups:

- **Understanding** — reading ontology content: class definitions, relations, properties, instances.
- **Reasoning** — symbolic inference: description logic, SWRL rules, constraint propagation.
- **Learning** — producing structure: generating definitions, building hierarchies, aligning ontologies.

Running 20 open models through it gives a consistent and fairly stark picture. On understanding, everything from 3B up scores in the seventies and eighties. On reasoning, scores drop. On learning, they collapse — most models score near zero on three of the five generation tasks, and the largest model in the study reaches 21.6 on the best of them. Scaling helps with understanding and barely moves learning.

Read the columns left to right and the story is the same at every scale: the U block is comfortable, the R block is workable, and the L block falls off a cliff. Whatever these models are doing with an ontology, it is not building one.
